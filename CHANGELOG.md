All notable changes to caw-ai. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- `caw design snapshot <test>@<S4> [--env <env>] [--name <name>] [--json]` and `caw_design` `{action: "snapshot", test, step, file?, env?}`: the screen at the entry of a step as a design page, the starting point for a draft in the app's own style. caw plays the test headless to the entry (like `open <test>@<S4>`, without window, chat or learning; it only reads the app) and writes `caw/design/<name>.html` (default `<test>-<S4>`): the DOM without scripts, event handlers and frames' content, field values frozen into attributes (never a password or a hidden field's value, the role's secrets redacted), animations and transitions at their end; and under `caw/design/assets/<name>/` the app's stylesheets as files (from the CSSOM: inserted rules kept, `@import` resolved; inline `<style>` stays in the page) with the fonts and images they and the page reference (same origin or already loaded by the page, read with GET from the page), `url()` rewritten relative (a page without any says so: "without external stylesheets or files", no folder is written). A snapshot onto a design a step links needs `personAsked: true`, like `put`; production is refused (`env-denied`), its screens hold real data. Design pages may now link files under `caw/design` relatively: `renderDesign` serves them from there, still without scripts and network. The judge and the comparison page measure the app once it settled (animations that end have ended, the DOM unchanged for 300 ms, at most 3 s). Measured: a snapshot judged against the same state is met with 0 deviations and renders offline with 0 differing pixels, on the errand example and on ExtJS 6.5 Modern with the Material theme (13 files, 1.6 MB: theme CSS, Roboto, Material Icons, Font Awesome); before the settling, a ripple and a moving label on ExtJS gave 10 and 12 false deviations. Guide topic `design` has "Draft from the app" as the default starting point; new e2e scenario N25 (snapshot, draft in its markup, red, built, met) on the native fixture.
- `caw_design` `put` with `stylesFrom: "design/<snapshot>.html"`: a draft's own `<style>` and `<link rel=stylesheet>` are replaced by the snapshot's stylesheets (paths relative to the draft), markup, classes and `style` attributes stay; the answer names how many were replaced and how many elements still carry a `style` attribute. The guide's "Take a draft from a Design artifact" converts an artboard drawn with the app's class names this way. Measured on errand (a new row, judged against the app built with its classes only): the artboard with its own CSS 61 deviations, with the app's class names and its own CSS 44, with `stylesFrom` 0; drafted from the snapshot 0.
- `data-caw-match="structure"` on an element of a design: inside it only presence, static texts, order and the relation of neighbours are compared, no looks (fonts, colors, backgrounds, paddings, radii, block borders and shadows), gaps, indents or sizes; outside it everything stays exact, the deviation lines are unchanged. `caw audit` names added structure areas in the `design-file-changed` line of a linked design. Measured on ExtJS: a draft in its own CSS 65 deviations, with the whole screen marked 5 (4 controls the app leaves without an accessible name, 1 label inside an empty field); swapped buttons, a changed title and a missing button are still caught.
- Decisions as data. Every card caw waits for has a stable id (`plan.approve`, `recording.adopt`, `red.proof`, `step.failed`, `effect.accept`, `target.use`, `look.adopt`, `design.unmet`, `check.failed`, `fix.guide`, `fix.green`, `advice.item`, `tokens`, `state.restore`, `record.start`, `app.dialog` and a few that only say what happened), a language-neutral `context` (test, step, reason, diagnosis, red step, files …) and options with stable ids, each with a gate: `free` when it changes no standard (close, later, discard, test again, record red, ask the agent), `human` when it does (approve a changed or writing plan, adopt a recording that changes what the test checks, accept an effect or a look, use a target, ignore errors, promote, apply app code, fix a step the app is blamed for). The waiting decision stands in the run folder (`decision.json`); `caw_status` shows it as `waiting.decision` with the state `deciding`. `result.json` lists every decision taken and who took it (`decisions`, `by`: `window`, `session`, `elicitation` or `auto`), `report.md` has a section "Decisions". The `next` of a draft (`caw_status`, `caw_reproductions`) names the window's card for its phase as `decision`. The cards in the window are unchanged.
- `caw record <test> --headless` (and `headless` with `record` in `caw_start`): a test that has a plan is recorded without a window, also in CI. The agent follows the plan; caw takes only what changes nothing the test checks: it approves the plan when it is the file's own (same steps and writing; a writing test only runs on a `writable` environment anyway), adopts the recording when it records that plan with no changed expected result, no message newly known (a step recorded for the first time sets its baseline, every step of a test's first recording, otherwise e.g. a pending step at the end: a newly known message seen only in such steps is adopted and named in `report.md` and by `caw audit`; seen in a step that had a recording before, it is the person's), no removed, fixed or stale step and no hard error on the way, saves a draft's red proof and closes cards that only say what happened. Anything else needs a person: the run stops there, writes nothing, names the open decision in `result.json` (`openDecision`, with its options and gates), `report.md` and `caw_status`, and exits `5` (`decision-needed` when it stopped at the plan). With an attached session the decision waits up to 10 minutes for the person's consent through `caw_decide`. Nobody demonstrates or steers without a window: a new test and `@<step>` are refused (exit 3), the agent cannot ask the person to show something, and no token notice is shown.
- `caw_decide` (MCP) answers the decision a run waits for, window or headless, like a button: `decision`, `option`, `run` (default the last run), `message` (only to revise a plan). A `free` option the session may answer; a `human` one only with the person's consent: caw asks them through the client with a form they confirm (MCP elicitation, `elicitation/create`; the model cannot answer it), and a client that did not declare that capability is refused with the way by hand (the window, or a run with one). The first answer wins between window and session; the window shows "answered by the session" (or "confirmed by you in the session"), and `result.json`, `report.md` and the terminal say who decided (`window`, `session`, `elicitation`, `terminal`, `auto`). `record --headless` started in a terminal (not CI, not by a session) asks a decision that needs a person right there: title, context and the options numbered, Enter for the one that changes nothing; the first answer wins against `caw_decide`, logged as `terminal`. `caw audit` lists, for information, the decisions this machine's runs took outside the window since the merge base that changed something (`decided` in `--json`). Only `caw_decide` can answer: the answer file counts only with an HMAC from a secret the run and the session's MCP server share (X25519, the private keys stay in the two processes); a run started through `caw_start` gets the server's key on its stdin and takes only its answers, a run started in a terminal takes those of the sessions listening in the project. An answer file without a valid proof, such as one the session wrote with its own file tools, is ignored and named in the terminal and in `report.md` ("invalid answer ignored", `ignoredAnswers` in `result.json`). Every MCP tool now refuses fields it does not take, naming the ones it does.
- The guided bug fix in the workbench and for your session. With a window the chat leads from the reproduced bug to the promoted test in one process, each phase in a new window with the chat so far, "Later" ending the run at any card, and a bar over each card showing the phases Reproduced › Red › Fix › Green › Promoted (done ones ticked, the current one highlighted): reproduced, **Record red** records the draft; saved red, **Now fix the bug** lists step, expected result, check (as code) and, when known, the suspected cause (from the reproduction's run folder, which `keepRuns` now keeps while its draft is in `caw/drafts`; the draft's first `notes` start with it for the recording agent) and offers **Let the agent fix it** (advice with every agent kind; code changes come as cards you apply one by one, caw's own agent never writes code), **I fix it myself** or later, then **Test again**; still red, fix again, test again or end; green, **Adopt** promotes the draft as `--promote` does, the chat names the fix report, a red neighbour test keeps the draft. `caw record <draft>` and `caw run <draft>` with a window join at their phase; the CLI, headless runs and CI stay as they were. Advice can name a cause outside the project (`side: "outside"` with `where`: another repository, a service, the environment): its card applies nothing, **I fix it there** waits for testing again. `caw_status` (for a reproduction and every run of a draft) and each row of `caw_reproductions` carry `phase` (`reproduced`, `red`, `green`, `promoted`) and `next` (record; fix the app with the red step, its check and cause, then run; promote; done with the fix report); the rows also carry `expected` and `cause`, and `reproduce.next` (the record command) is gone from `caw_status`.
- Guided target-image TDD: a draft of a new screen built test-first like a bug fix, in the phases Draft › Red › Build › Green › Adopted. A design draft is `caw/drafts/<id>.yaml` with `origin: design` (only in a draft; `validate` refuses it in `caw/tests` and any other value), written by your session like a test plan: the steps up to the screen, recorded like a test (no failing check required), and at the end the step the design is for, linked with `caw_design` `link`, which now also finds a draft. Its red step is the last step with `design:`. Red is a run that judged that step's design not met with every step before it passed: at its entry while the step is pending (the feature cannot be walked yet), or after its passed check once it is recorded (failing with kind `design`, or only warning with `design.fail` without `target`); `run` and `record` save it as `red: { step, run, head }`, also without a window. A new plan keeps that `red` while the red step keeps its design and expected result, so recording the pending step once the design is met keeps the proof. Green is a later run that judged it met; `run --promote` (and **Adopt**) adopts it only with the design met, moves the test without `origin` and `red` and writes `design-report.md` (red at which step with how many deviations in the red run, run and commit; green in which run; the changed files; the other tests green). In the window the same cards as the bug fix, worded for the design (the same decisions and gates: `red.proof`, `fix.guide`, `fix.green` human): the red proof, **Now build the target design** with the deviations as rows and **Let Claude Code build it** (the agent gets `task.fix` with the design and the deviations) / **I build it myself** / **Later**, **Target design met: S3** with **Adopt**, and for a design met at a pending step's entry a card to record it first (`design.record`); the phase bar shows Draft › Red › Build › Green › Adopted. For your session `caw_status`, `caw_reproductions` and now `caw_list` (each draft) carry `origin: design`, `phase` (`draft`, `red`, `green`, `adopted`) and `next`: record headless, `link-design`, run headless, `build-design` with the step, design and deviations, record the pending step, run with `promote`, done with `designReport`. Guide topic `design` has the recipe "Build a draft with TDD" (named in topics `session` and `overview` and in the `caw-attach` skill), topic `advise` the task. New e2e scenario N24 follows it on the native fixture.
- The visual layer, on the same test as the target design. `caw open <test>@S3 --design` plays through S3 and opens the comparison with its design, a page of its own over the workbench (F7 opens and closes it, Esc closes it, open or closed survives reloads; the app stays below it as it is): a header with the way back, a narrow rail of the steps that have a design with their status (met, N deviations, pending, not compared), unfolding to their names; the step with its design file, size, run and **met** or **not met**; the tabs **Side by side** (app and design at the recorded size, 100 % or down to 75 % to fit the width, from the left edge, scrolling together; when a column would need less than 75 %, one above the other in the full width, scrolling sideways only when even that needs less; a wide window keeps them side by side), **Overlaid** (the design half transparent over the app) and **Differences only** (all but the deviating elements faded); the deviations as numbered cards with their kind (Missing, Text, Style, Layout) that mark their element with its number in both images and scroll both down to it, sideways only when it is out of view (`compareDetailed` gives every deviation the element's box in the design and in the app); **Ask Claude Code to build it** asks in the chat as the card of a design not met does (`design.unmet`, `ask`). The app side is a capture of the window's app frame at the recorded size, taken when the comparison is computed; from the session `caw_start` with `design: true`. - Renamed targets repaired with proof, without AI: when a click, double click, fill or type finds its element gone and exactly one similar element is there (button "Speichern" now "Sichern"), caw tries that one with every write aborted, if the action's effect is learned and does not write. Proven only if the learned effect comes again, nothing tried to write and the step's unchanged check passes. The step still fails with kind `target` and carries `proposal: { step, action, from, to, evidence }` in `result.json` ("Proposed targets" in `report.md`); the replay goes on. The chat button **Use "Sichern"** changes only that target in the recording (hash, description and learned effects stay) and runs again; in CI only the report. `caw audit` lists such a target change (`locator-changed`), not as a weakening.
- From a reproduced bug to a proven fix: `caw reproduce` writes a reproduced bug (`yes`) as a draft to `caw/drafts/<id>.yaml`, plan and later its recording in one file, with Claude's browser actions and their locators in its `notes`; no and unclear leave no draft, and nothing lands in the run folder as `draft-test.yaml` any more. Drafts are outside `run --all`; `list`, `validate`, `caw_list` and `caw_validate` show them apart. `caw record <draft>` records it with its last step required red: the recorder keeps exactly one failing check for it, as long as it evaluated, and the check run shows "S3 red as expected: the test catches the bug" when every step before passed and S3 failed at its check; saving keeps `red: { step, run, head }` (only drafts may carry it). `caw run <draft>` replays it: still red it says so and offers no fix, green it names the next command. `caw run <draft> --promote` (and `promote` in `caw_start`) needs the draft green and `run --all` on the same environment green, then moves the test to `caw/tests` and `caw/recordings`, deletes the draft and writes `fix-report.md` into the run folder (red at which step, run and commit; green in which run; the files changed since: tracked files in the whole repository, untracked ones only inside the project folder and never an untracked folder such as a nested repository, without `caw/` and `.caw/`; how many other tests passed). Exit 0 promoted, 1 not green, 4 not recorded or never red.
- `caw bisect <test> --env <env> [--tests-from <ref>]` for `git bisect run`: finds the commit that broke a test. The test, its recording and `caw.config.json` come from the ref (default `refs/bisect/bad`), since bisect checks out `caw/` too. Each commit starts the environment with its `start` command, plays the test headless without chat and without learning (the tree stays clean), with no learned effect failing a step, and prints one line (commit, result, assessment, exit). Exit `0` good, `1` bad (a step failed on the app), `125` skip (environment, test, unclear, any error), `128` stops the bisect (invalid call, test or config at the ref, test not recorded or stale, no `start`, or the environment already answers before caw starts it). Run it from a fixed install: `git bisect run npx -p caw-ai@<version> caw bisect …`.
- Target designs: a step can name the screen it should end on (`design: design/<name>.html`) and what must be on the page before it can be recorded (`needs:` with locators). Only people write both into the test file; they are outside the step's hash (changing them never makes a step stale), Claude's plan is refused when it carries them, and caw keeps them when it rewrites a step (fix, re-record from a step, whole re-record). After a step's check passed, a run renders its `.html` design in a separate headless browser without scripts or network at the step's recorded viewport and compares controls, view lines and per control font-size, font-weight, color, background-color, border-radius and padding with the app's page, leaving out list rows, free text and numbers. Not met, the step fails with kind `design`: the replay goes on, without retry and without learning effects for that step; the chat offers to have it built by the attached session, to open the step with its design (**Open with design**, option `open-design`: the window of `open <test>@S<n> --design` in the same process with the chat so far; the run keeps its exit code) or to close, never to fix the step or forget its effects. `design.fail: []` in the config only warns. `result.json` holds `design: { file, verdict, missing, extra, styleDiffs }` per judged step; at the end of a healthy run the first pending step's `needs` are checked and the run says "S4 is ready to record: caw record <test>@S4 --env <env>" or lists what is missing (`needs` in `result.json`). The new MCP tool `caw_design` lets the session `put` a design into `caw/design` (.html, .png, DTCG tokens.json; name, size, PNG signature and dimensions checked, no links, nothing outside), `list` designs and the steps that link them, and `link` one step's `design:` when the person asks; guide topic `design`. `examples/errand` has a design with tokens.
- Tickets from another tool, while caw stays out of ticket systems: `caw reproduce --ref <id>` (and `ref` in `caw_start`) takes an opaque id (up to 64 letters, digits and `. _ # : / -`, otherwise exit 3) that caw never looks up; it stands in `report.md`, `result.json` (`reproduce.ref`), `caw_status` and the draft's notes. A session that has an MCP server serving tickets chains it with `caw_start` per ticket. The new MCP tool `caw_reproductions` lists the reproductions in `.caw/runs` as data, newest first (`runId`, `date`, `ref`, `status`, `reproduced`, `observed`, `covered`, `draft`, `report`), filtered by `refs` or `since`, and names refs without a run as `missing` (never run, or pruned by `keepRuns`).
- Claude's reproduction verdict may name an existing test that already covers the reported behaviour (`covered: { test, step?, why }`). caw accepts only a recorded test (and step) and adds its newest run from `.caw/runs` (status, run, date); if that run passed while the bug may be there, the report says its check may miss the bug (`checkMayMiss`).
- `caw audit <ref> [--json]`: no test gets weaker unnoticed. Compares `caw/tests`, `caw/recordings` and `caw.config.json` at the merge base of `<ref>` and `HEAD` with the working tree and prints Markdown in the team language for a PR comment. Weakened (exit 1): a check changed while its expected result stayed (matched by the expected result, a moved step is fine), a test or step removed, an unreadable test file, fewer environments, `retry` added or raised, a step or action timeout raised, an action made optional, `knownMessages` added, learned effects dropped from the fail lists, console lines taken in or effects dropped, and in the config more `consoleIgnore`, `foreignCode` or `reads`, fewer `messageSelectors`, a narrower `effects.fail` or a guarded key changed. A changed expected result (the way to change a check on purpose) and new tests and steps are listed; changed request baselines and pruned warn-only effects are for information. Exit 3 for an unknown ref or without Git (`git-ref`, `git-missing`).
- CI with triage: JUnit types a failed step by caw's assessment (`<failure type="app|environment|test|unclear" message="S3: <reason>">` with the findings and the report path as text) and anything else by its error code (`<error type="replay-stale">`, instead of `fail` and `needs-recording`). `--report` is repeatable: `summary=<file>` writes one Markdown line per test for `$GITHUB_STEP_SUMMARY`, `json=<file>` the same for scripts with steps and attempts. `--retry-env <n>` runs a test again, at most `n` times, only while it fails on the environment (exit 2, or the assessment `environment`); the last attempt counts and the reports say e.g. "passed on attempt 2 (environment)". A failed step still exits 1 whatever the assessment. `CAW_URL__<ENV>` points an environment at a PR preview for one process, named in the run's first line and never written to the config; refused for production and agent environments and a production host (`url-override`, exit 3).
- Pending steps: steps written at the end of a test but not recorded yet no longer block it. `run` plays the recorded steps and passes, the terminal, `report.md` and `result.json` (`pending`, and `status: "pending"` under `steps`) name them, the workbench shows them as not reached, `list` shows `pending (S4, S5)`, `caw_list` and `caw_validate` report them. `run --all` counts such a test as passed, JUnit carries the note in `<system-out>`. `run --strict` (`strict` in `caw_start`) exits 4 while anything is pending (`replay-pending`). `open <test>@S4` with S4 the first pending step lands after S3; behind it `open` names the step to record first. A gap (unrecorded step before a recorded one), a step changed after recording and a test with nothing recorded still exit 4.
- Recording a whole test again (`record <test>`, `@S1`) keeps the rule of fixing a step: a step whose expected result stays must keep its check, also when it moved; a changed expected result is shown at the decision.
- `result.json` lists every step under `steps`: status, why it failed (`kind`), its time, its requests and the effects of its actions with any deviation from the learned ones (on production without effects).
- `caw reproduce "<bug report>"` (also `--file`, or `-` for stdin): Claude reproduces a bug on its own, signed in as a test role, optionally from a test's state (`--from <test>@S3`, those steps replayed without Claude). No plan to approve, no question, no advice and no code change; Claude ends with reproduced yes, no or unclear, observed against expected, the steps, screenshots and a hint at the cause. The run folder gets `report.md` and `draft-test.yaml`, a test plan you can adopt and record; `caw/tests` stays untouched. Only on environments with `agent`, never production (`reproduce-not-allowed`), with the browser's network lock; without `writable` every write request is blocked. Exit `0` reproduced, `1` not or unclear, `2` environment (`reproduce-state` when `--from` does not reach its state). From Claude Code: `caw_start` with `command: "reproduce"`, `caw_status` names verdict, report and draft.

Read-only and without secrets: the app shares the origin. `caw.help()` lists them.

### Security
- The page under test can no longer act as you in the workbench. caw writes the chat only into a chat frame that still has its first document and an opaque origin, checked in a script the page cannot touch; if the page removes the chat's sandbox or reloads it, the chat closes and the run ends with `chat-tampered` (exit 2). In a demonstration the page reports only the clicks, entries, uploads and keys the recorder captures itself, never notes, checkpoints, checks or ready-made targets. Keys go only to the application, not to the chat; the application frame is known by identity, not by a name the page can change. Only the application frame's own documents lose `X-Frame-Options` and `frame-ancestors`. The page may read the clipboard only while a check, `remember` or `eval` runs.
- While Claude is attached (record, a run with chat, `open`), the browser reaches only the environments, `allowHosts` and the new `thirdPartyHosts` (top level and per environment); every other host is unresolvable in Chrome and named as a warning in the run, fetch, WebSockets, beacons, iframes and redirect hops included. The Docker firewall never covered the browser; README and guide now say so.
- Chrome always runs in its sandbox; where it cannot start, caw stops with `browser-sandbox` and names the unsafe opt-out `browser.noSandbox`. The workbench no longer turns off Local Network Access; the permission goes to the application's origin only.
- Production: hosts stay blocked when written with a trailing dot or in another case; write requests are blocked to any host, sign-in requests pass only while caw signs in, and a form post into the workbench's top window is refused too. Request names keep a value with spaces as one token, and a JSON body counts as GraphQL only with GraphQL fields. WebSocket messages and production reached by IP address remain documented limits.
- Overlay: the dev server answers and bridges only the environment and itself; integrity is removed only from the application's own tags; a local build no longer serves names containing a backslash, colon or NUL.
- Everything Claude gets is masked, error messages, URLs and dialog texts included; errors arrive without Playwright's call log, and no command runs while caw signs in again. A failed sign-in fill names the field, never the value; a gate's Basic credentials are masked in `trace.zip`. README says honestly what masking can and cannot do against a misbehaving agent.
- `caw_config` no longer changes the commands caw runs (`start`, `stop`, `reset`, `ready`, `lintCommand`, overlay `start`), the environment flags (`production`, `agent`, `writable`, `allowHttp`, `insecureTls`, also by adding or removing an environment), `browser.executablePath`, `sandboxImage`, `sandboxUser` or `agent.mode`: the session makes those edits with its own Edit tool, so you see the diff. `--report junit` and `caw_init` `fromExplore` stay inside the project.
- Your Claude connection settings no longer land in the project: a private temporary file, deleted after the run, and the env values in the process environment. On Windows Claude Code is looked up on the PATH only, never in the current folder. `browser.executablePath` refuses network and `/proc` paths. - The `.mcp.json` entry pins npx to this version and looks for an installed copy only up to the repository root. A session marker counts only with a token kept outside the project. - The release workflow publishes from a separate job that alone holds the OIDC token, with npm 11.15.0 and install scripts off; CI runs with read-only permissions. The docs say that `caw.config.json` and recordings (`check`, `remember`) are executable configuration to review like code.

## [1.0.0-rc.1] - 2026-11-18

First release candidate for 1.0.

### Added
- Test plans as YAML in `caw/tests/` (steps `S<n>` with title, action and one expected result) and recordings per step in `caw/recordings/`, written by the tool only.
- `caw record`: you demonstrate, describe a new test in the chat (each message keeps its page; clicks are not recorded), Claude follows the plan, or the Claude Code session records. Your messages are numbered H1, H2 …; the plan says in `heard` what became of each, and the approval card shows your words with it; every recording ends with a plan you approve and a check run. `record <test>@S4` re-records from a step to the end.
- Recordings store native Playwright locators, one per target (`getByRole('button', { name: 'Save', exact: true })`, frames via `contentFrame()`), parsed without running code; ExtJS components also via the `extjs=` selector engine (`Ext.ComponentQuery`). Replay needs exactly one visible, uncovered match, tolerates two for a dialog transition, scrolls virtual lists and explains a missing target (frame missing, covered by a dialog, renamed).
- Actions `click`, `dblclick`, `fill`, `type`, `press`, `upload`, `download`; a demonstrated double-click is recorded as one `dblclick`, a file chosen from `caw/fixtures` as an `upload` (files from elsewhere are named, never copied). A plan reworded during recording keeps the checks already passed; only a changed expected result or order takes them back, and going back to it restores them.
- `caw run`: deterministic replay without Claude, with retries for flaky pages, learned network and effect baselines, and a split between test, application and environment failures. Several tests in a row (`run a b c`, `run --all`), JUnit output, CI mode (`--no-chat`, `--headless`, `--claude-timeout`, `--max-actions`).
- `caw open`: the app signed in with workbench and chat; `open <test>` replays the test and hands over the window, `open <test>@S4` stops at the entry of S4.
- Workbench: steps, the app under its own address and the chat in one window; key bar (F1 Open…, F5 Reload, F9 Pause, Esc Cancel, F2 Requests, F3 Console, F4 Steps, F6 Chat, F7 Theme, F8 App only, F10 Animations off).
- Fixing and developing in the chat: ask Claude, fix a step, get code advice to accept or reject; while developing Claude can use the page and propose code changes.
- Extensions outside the core, documented in `docs/extensions/`: the overlay (your local code over a deployed environment, per part from its code server or a build folder; `caw overlay status` and `overlay check`, `--overlay` on `run`, `record`, `open`) is active only with an `overlay` block in `caw.config.json`, otherwise help, validate, doctor and the MCP tools leave it out and `caw overlay`/`--overlay` name the page. ExtJS (`"framework": "extjs"`, written by `init` for a Sencha workspace) adds the `extjs=` locators, its quiet-page probe, message boxes, the Sencha watch as overlay source and the `extjs` guide topic.
- Setup: `init` (`--explore-only` only looks at the app), `validate`, `doctor`, `role` (`role login` stores an SSO session), `list`; the MCP server (`caw mcp`) lets your session set up projects, start runs and act as the agent.
- Agents: `local` (Claude Code with narrow tools), `docker` (isolated sandbox with firewall) and `session` (your Claude Code session); cost limits via `agent.model` and `agent.maxBudgetUsd`.
- Sign-in: login form, one-time codes, steps on an identity provider (`init --explore-only` suggests them from the provider's pages, identifier-first and one-time codes included), SSO via saved sessions; test users per role in `caw/.env` or the environment, filled in by hand.
- Network and safety: one request name (`POST /graphql mutation CloseTicket`, `json.<key>` for JSON-RPC) and one rule for what writes, with `reads` for requests that only read; pages navigate only to the app's hosts and `allowHosts` (`host-blocked`), while scripts, fonts, analytics and iframes may load from anywhere and are listed as third-party hosts whose errors are warnings; production hosts are unreachable from other environments, and a run on production sends no write requests (`write-blocked`); error pages count as evidence.
- Environment lifecycle: `start`, `stop`, `reset` and `ready` per environment with your own commands. `--start` starts an environment that doesn't answer and stops it at the end (already running: used, never stopped); `run` resets once before the first writing test, `--reset` before the first test, `--no-reset` never; error codes `env-start-failed`, `env-not-ready`, `env-reset-failed` and `env-unreachable`. Never on production.
- One language per project: caw's own texts follow the team language (`language`, if `de` or `en`) unless you choose your own with `CAW_LANG`; names from the app and the config stand in straight quotes in every language.
- Learning only on request: `run --learn` writes missing baselines and effects in every mode; with chat a passed run offers "Learn baselines" on its end card; otherwise a run changes no file. The mode line names environment, team language and whether the run learns.
- `caw export <test>`: the recorded test as a Playwright test (`<test>.spec.mjs`, `caw-support.mjs`, `playwright.config.mjs` with the list and HTML reporters) that runs without caw, with the recorded locators, dialog decisions, `remember` and placeholders.
- Claude Code setup through MCP only: `claude mcp add caw --scope project -- npx caw-ai mcp`. The server is named `caw`, tells Claude what caw is for and serves the guidance per topic with `caw_guide`. `caw_init` (`explore`, `write`, `offline`) and `caw_role` (`add`, `login`) set up the project from the session. `init` points an `npx` entry at the installed copy once caw is in `node_modules`.
- Native dialogs (alert, confirm, prompt, beforeunload): the answer is recorded on the action that opens the dialog and replayed; unexpected dialogs are dismissed and reported (`dialog-unexpected`), missing ones too (`dialog-missing`). The chat asks while you demonstrate; Claude arms the answer with `caw agent dialog`.
- Actions `hover` and `dragTo`.
- Dates as `{{date±n}}` in the browser's locale and `{{isoDate±n}}`; `remember` takes values of any length, short ones replaced only where they cannot be other text.
- The workbench shows the app at the recorded viewport, scaled down to fit, while caw replays; once the window is handed over the app fills the pane.
- The step list has a heading "Run" ("Durchführung") above the test's steps, between preparation and wrap-up.
- Themes as packages: built-in `studio` (default), `terminal`, `raster` and `glas`, each with a dark and a light mode; own themes next to `prefs.json` (their `light` block holds the light colours), `caw theme` with `use`, `mode` and `check`. F7 in the workbench picks the theme and switches between dark and light at once, a mode the theme lacks greyed out; it never edits a theme, own themes are folders written by hand. The terminal keeps the dark colours. Small labels use the text font in every theme, because the pixel font VT323 is never pixel-exact at small sizes; it stays for the logo and, in `terminal`, for large type.
- The command is `caw` (package `caw-ai`, `npx caw …` once installed). Claude's browser commands live under `caw agent` and are not in the human help. Durations carry their unit (`--step-timeout 30s`, `--claude-timeout 60m`); `--env` picks the environment, `--explain` lets Claude explain failures unasked, `--max-actions` limits Claude. Old command and flag names are refused with their new name.
- User interface, CLI, messages and reports in English and German.
